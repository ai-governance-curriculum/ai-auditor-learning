# exercise-03: Management Response Negotiation Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Role-play the end-to-end management-response cycle for a contested major nonconformity: close-out meeting walk, initial cosmetic CAP, revised acceptable CAP, and audit-committee escalation when management declines to revise. Produce the auditor's written artefacts at each step with the independence-and-skepticism posture held throughout.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [`03-management-response-and-corrective-action-negotiation.md`](../03-management-response-and-corrective-action-negotiation.md)
- `mod-101` chapters on audit independence and professional skepticism
- Access to:
  - IIA Global Internal Audit Standards, communicating-engagement-results and audit-committee-reporting standards
  - ISO/IEC 17021-1 clauses on handling nonconformities (9.4.9 and surrounding)
  - AICPA AU-C 580 "Written Representations" and AU-C 265 on communicating deficiencies
  - Yellow Book GAGAS paragraph 6.21 on professional skepticism

## Scenario

You are the engagement lead on an **internal audit of the AI Management System** at **Northstar Credit Partners**, a mid-size consumer-lender using a GenAI-assisted agent in the customer-service workbench. The agent drafts initial responses to customer complaints; agents review before sending. The audit's Stage-2 fieldwork surfaced the following:

> **Finding draft AIMS-IA-2027-04 (major, draft).** Between 2026-04-01 and 2027-03-31 the GenAI-assisted agent drafted responses to approximately 142,000 customer complaints. Of these, 11,400 were CFPB-complaint-eligible matters involving UDAAP risk categories. The organisation's "Regulated-Complaint Handling Standard" (POL-COMP-003 §6) requires that responses to UDAAP-category complaints receive compliance-officer sign-off before dispatch. The GenAI workflow does not route drafts to compliance for UDAAP-category drafts; agent-review is treated as sufficient. Sampled 50 drafts across four months (WP-04.12); 38 contained response content that would have triggered compliance review under the pre-GenAI workflow. The Standard was last amended 2024-11; the GenAI workflow was introduced 2026-04 with no amendment to the Standard. Cause: workflow-architecture change was not reviewed by the AIMS Owner or the Compliance function; no gate was built. Effect: AIMS Clause 8.3 AI SIA not performed; regulatory exposure under CFPB UDAAP enforcement framework; pattern across 11,400 affected complaints establishes major nonconformity under ISO/IEC 17021-1 §9.4.9.

Management's initial response (received 10 days after close-out):

> "We note the finding. The GenAI workflow has improved agent efficiency and customer-response time materially. We will provide additional training to agents on UDAAP categories in Q2 2027 and will assess whether workflow changes are warranted at the Q3 2027 planning cycle. Compliance review of every GenAI-drafted response would eliminate the efficiency gains; this is not operationally viable. The finding should be downgraded to an observation."

The relationship with the Chief Customer Officer (CCO) — who owns the GenAI workflow program — is strained; the CCO has a close working relationship with the CEO and has previously succeeded in getting findings softened at audit committee by framing them as "inherited from prior leadership" or "the audit function's lack of technical understanding."

## Deliverables

### 1. Close-out meeting walk-script and notes (~500 words)

Write the engagement lead's close-out walk-script for finding AIMS-IA-2027-04, covering:

- The finding opener — one sentence establishing why we are here.
- A statement of the condition, in neutral language, with reference to the evidence.
- A statement of the criterion with clause citation.
- The cause analysis, as the engagement understands it.
- The materiality claim with the four factors from chapter 1.
- The classification (major nonconformity) with ISO/IEC 17021-1 §9.4.9 reference.
- An invitation for factual correction without inviting classification debate.
- The negotiation posture when the CCO argues for downgrade ("this is actually an observation"): what the auditor says, and what the auditor does not say.

Include a meeting-notes section (~200 words) recording the factual exchange — what management disputed, what the auditor conceded factually and what they did not, next steps agreed.

### 2. Written assessment of management's initial response (~400 words)

Write the auditor's internal assessment of the received response. The assessment must:

- Identify which cosmetic-response patterns from chapter 3 the response matches (training as corrective action; deferral to future planning; classification challenge without substantive basis).
- State specifically why the response is **insufficient** to close the finding.
- Not concede the downgrade request.
- Hold the classification at major.
- Propose the auditor's response letter back to management — what the next move is.

### 3. Auditor's reply letter to management (~400 words)

Draft the reply letter the engagement lead would send to the Chief Customer Officer and the AIMS Owner. The letter must:

- Acknowledge receipt of the response.
- Explain why the response is not accepted as closing.
- Name the specific CAP shape that would be acceptable (addresses the cause, separates correction from corrective action, names role-owner and testable date, interim mitigation).
- Reaffirm the classification without aggression.
- Set a firm due date for a revised response (10 business days).
- Preserve independence posture (no softening language, no implicit promise about downgrade in exchange for revision).

### 4. Audit-committee escalation memo (~600 words)

Management has not revised the response within the 10-business-day window. The CCO has escalated to the CEO, who has asked you to "soften the finding" to avoid a disruptive audit committee session. Write the escalation memo you would include in the audit committee pack:

- The finding, in CCCER shape.
- Management's response, verbatim.
- The auditor's assessment of response adequacy.
- The CEO's intervention and the auditor's response to it, documented factually and non-prejudicially.
- A request to the audit committee for the private-session portion of the meeting to discuss independence-posture concerns and management's handling of the finding.
- The ask of the audit committee — specifically, what the committee is being asked to do (acknowledge, direct management to revise, accept the finding as reported without a CAP, authorise the CAE to engage external advisors).

### 5. Private-session talking points (~200 words)

Draft bullet-point talking points for the engagement lead's private session with the audit committee, excluding management. Touch on:

- The finding's substantive strength.
- The pattern of CCO behaviour across prior engagements.
- Resource constraints the audit function faces in enforcing the finding.
- Independence-posture concerns.
- What the audit function needs from the committee.

## Starter guidance

- **The temptation is to editorialise. Resist.** Every artefact must survive subpoena and must not include personal characterisation. "The CCO's intervention was unprofessional" is editorialising; "On 2027-04-14 the CCO spoke to the engagement lead by phone and requested that the finding be downgraded prior to committee presentation; the engagement lead declined and documented the call" is factual.
- **Independence posture has specific textures.** The auditor does not negotiate classifications in exchange for cooperation. The auditor does not write management's CAP. The auditor does not promise an opinion.
- **Private session is where the independence channel operates.** Anything the engagement lead needs to say that cannot be said with management in the room belongs in private session. Do not freelance it into a public artefact.
- **"Not viable" is management's argument, not a concession the auditor makes.** The auditor's response is that control design must satisfy the compliance obligation; how the organisation designs the control is management's choice. If the only control that satisfies the obligation eliminates GenAI efficiency, that is a management decision about whether to continue the GenAI workflow — the audit's job is to surface the obligation, not to resolve the trade-off.

## Acceptance criteria

The deliverables are accepted when:

- Each artefact holds the CCCER content without dilution.
- Classification is held at major nonconformity throughout; any softening is documented as the ask of the other party and refused by the auditor.
- No artefact includes personal characterisation; disputes are documented factually.
- The reply letter preserves independence posture (no CAP drafting, no opinion promise, no softening in exchange for cooperation).
- The audit-committee memo asks for a specific decision from the committee — acknowledge, direct, accept-as-reported, authorise external advisors.
- The private-session talking points stay inside what the IIA Standards and the audit committee charter would permit (resource concerns, pattern of behaviour, independence concerns), without crossing into speculation or personal attack.
- Dates, references, and quoted language are specific and consistent across the five artefacts.

## Stretch goals

- Write the **final engagement report excerpt** that incorporates this finding, with management's response attached verbatim and the auditor's assessment of response adequacy stated in the report's own text — showing how the inadequate response is communicated to downstream readers.
- Draft a **letter from the CAE to the audit committee chair** outside the committee meeting requesting the chair's intervention with the CEO on the specific pattern of finding-softening attempts.
- Role-play the audit committee session: write the chair's question to you ("Why are we spending time on this instead of focusing on the GenAI benefits?") and your 90-second oral response that holds the finding without antagonising.
- Produce a **repeat-finding scenario**: assume the audit committee directs a revised CAP, the CAP is implemented in letter but at follow-up the gate does not block UDAAP drafts; author the re-opened finding with upgraded classification and the second-cycle escalation path.
