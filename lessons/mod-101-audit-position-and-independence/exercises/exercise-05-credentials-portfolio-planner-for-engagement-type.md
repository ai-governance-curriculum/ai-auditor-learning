# exercise-05: Credentials Portfolio Planner for Engagement Type

**Estimated effort:** 1.5 hours

## Objective

Produce an engagement-driven credentials plan for an AI auditor career trajectory, justifying every credential by the engagements it unlocks. The deliverable is a one-page credentials map plus a two-year acquisition plan with cost and time estimates — the kind of document a practitioner would show their manager when negotiating a professional-development budget.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [`07-credentials-portfolio.md`](../07-credentials-portfolio.md)
- Chapter [`01-third-line-scope-and-boundaries.md`](../01-third-line-scope-and-boundaries.md) (for engagement-type definitions)

## Scenario

Pick **one** of the following practitioner profiles. Author the credentials plan for that profile.

### Profile A — Big-4 AI Assurance senior manager (US, promotion track toward partner)

- Current: licensed CPA, CISA, 8 years in IT audit (3 in AI/ML engagements), currently a senior manager in a Big-4 Risk Assurance practice in the US.
- Immediate engagements: AT-C 205 examinations for enterprise AI programs; SOC 2 Type II with AI criteria; preparing to lead the firm's AI-governance advisory practice.
- Three-year target: partner-track; engagements expand to include ISO/IEC 42001 pre-cert advisory and NYC LL144 bias audits.

### Profile B — Internal Audit AI Practice lead at a large US bank

- Current: CIA, CISA, 10 years in bank internal audit (4 in technology audit, 2 of which in model-risk audit under SR 11-7).
- Immediate engagements: internal audits of the bank's AI/ML governance program under IIA Standards; coordinating with the bank's MRM second line; audit-committee reporting on AI governance maturity.
- Three-year target: expand scope to cover the bank's third-party AI (GPAI providers), interact with Federal Reserve / OCC exams on AI topics.

### Profile C — Independent AI auditor at a boutique firm (UK)

- Current: ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Auditor, prior background in privacy (CIPP/E), 6 years as a privacy/security consultant now moving into AI assurance; the firm practises under ISAE 3000 and holds a UK accreditation for ISO/IEC 27001 certification.
- Immediate engagements: ISO/IEC 42001 pre-certification advisory for UK/EU clients; limited-assurance ISAE 3000 engagements on AI governance programs; EU AI Act readiness reviews.
- Three-year target: become an accredited CB auditor for ISO/IEC 42001 certification; lead ForHumanity IAAIS engagements on EU AI Act readiness.

### Profile D — AI auditor at a notified body (continental Europe)

- Current: ISO 13485 + ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Auditor, 7 years auditing medical-device management systems, now moving into AI audit as the CB expands into ISO/IEC 42001 and prepares for EU AI Act notified-body designation.
- Immediate engagements: ISO/IEC 42001 certification audits; preparation for the CB's EU AI Act Article 43 third-party conformity assessment designation.
- Three-year target: lead auditor on EU AI Act conformity-assessment engagements.

## Deliverables

### 1. Credentials map (one page, Markdown or diagram)

For the chosen profile, show:

- Current credentials (already held).
- **Load-bearing** credentials for the immediate engagements — those without which the practitioner cannot lead or sign the engagement.
- **Market-expected** credentials — those a buyer or regulator would expect even if not strictly required.
- **Nice-to-have** credentials — signal value, portfolio depth, cross-selling.

For each credential, list: credentialing body, prerequisite (if any), typical acquisition effort (study hours / exam cost / experience hours), continuing-education / CPD annual commitment, engagement types it unlocks.

### 2. Two-year acquisition plan (Markdown table)

A quarter-by-quarter acquisition plan over 8 quarters, each quarter listing:

- Study/exam activities planned.
- Expected credential completion date.
- Estimated time commitment (hours).
- Estimated cost (exam, training, membership).
- Engagement-portfolio impact at completion.

### 3. Justification memo (~400 words)

For each credential in the plan, in one sentence, justify it by the specific engagement types it unlocks. Flag any credential whose justification is "signal value only" explicitly — the reader will accept signal-value credentials, but will not accept them disguised as load-bearing.

## Starter guidance

- The question "load-bearing vs. market-expected vs. nice-to-have" is the hinge. Many practitioners accidentally over-invest in signal-value credentials (badge collection) while missing a load-bearing one for the engagements they actually run.
- Continuing-education commitments are real — a portfolio with 6 credentials each requiring 20+ hours/year of CPD is 120+ hours/year before you do any audit work. Model this honestly.
- Costs vary by region; placeholder figures are acceptable as long as they are realistic (not off by an order of magnitude).
- Cross-profile reading: look at the credentials in two profiles you did NOT pick and note how the load-bearing set differs — this helps you defend why your plan is right for your chosen profile.
- Do NOT invent credential names, prerequisites, or structures. Where a credential's structure has changed recently or is in transition, mark `needs-research` and cite the source you checked.

## Acceptance criteria

The deliverables are accepted when:

- The credentials map clearly distinguishes load-bearing, market-expected, and nice-to-have tiers.
- Every credential is attributed to a credentialing body and includes prerequisite, effort, cost, and annual CPD estimates.
- The two-year plan sequences credentials sensibly (prerequisites first; concurrent study loads realistic).
- Each credential in the plan has a one-sentence engagement-justification.
- At least one credential is explicitly marked "signal value only" if any are included on that basis.
- No credential is invented or materially misdescribed; unverifiable claims are marked `needs-research`.

## Stretch goals

- Author a **companion plan** for a *different* profile (one of the other three above) and write a 200-word comparison of the two plans — what differs, what is shared, what you learned.
- Produce a **buyer-side reading guide** — a one-page memo a CAE or managing partner would use to read a candidate's credentials portfolio during hiring. Which credentials make them an immediate yes, which are green flags, which are yellow flags (missing load-bearing credential for the engagements the role expects).
- Argue the **"no, I would not pursue credential X"** case for one high-profile credential that most practitioners pursue by default — defend the opportunity-cost reasoning.
