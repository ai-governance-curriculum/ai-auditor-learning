# ISO 19011, ISO/IEC 17021-1, and ISO/IEC 42006 — the Management-System Audit Shape

## Motivation

ISO/IEC 42001 certification audits are not IIA engagements and are not IAASB/AICPA attestations. They run under their own professional stack: **ISO 19011** for the audit-practice fundamentals, **ISO/IEC 17021-1** for the certification-body operating requirements, and **ISO/IEC 42006** as the AIMS-specific overlay on top of 17021-1. If you execute, support, or audit alongside an ISO/IEC 42001 certification, these three documents shape every move — stage-1, stage-2, surveillance, recertification. This chapter installs them.

## ISO 19011:2018 — guidelines for auditing management systems

**ISO 19011:2018** is the horizontal **guideline** (not requirement) for auditing management systems. Where ISO 17021-1 binds a certification body, ISO 19011 is the guide every management-system auditor — internal, second-party, first-party — reaches for to actually structure the work.

### Seven principles (keep these at the front of mind)

ISO 19011 opens with seven principles of auditing, which every subsequent clause operationalises:

1. **Integrity** — the professional foundation.
2. **Fair presentation** — audit findings, conclusions, and reports reflect truthfully and accurately audit activities.
3. **Due professional care** — application of diligence and judgment.
4. **Confidentiality** — security of information.
5. **Independence** — basis for impartiality and objectivity.
6. **Evidence-based approach** — rational method for reaching reliable and reproducible audit conclusions.
7. **Risk-based approach** — audit considers risks and opportunities.

Internalise these. In an AIMS audit, a certification body QA reviewer and an accreditation body reviewer both read your workpapers looking for operation of these seven principles by name.

### Audit programme vs. audit

ISO 19011 distinguishes:

- The **audit programme** — the set of audits planned for a specific time frame and directed toward a specific purpose. For an internal AIMS audit programme, the organisation plans multi-year coverage of all AIMS clauses + AI systems in scope.
- An **individual audit** — one engagement against a scope. Each audit has planning, conducting, reporting, completion, and follow-up phases.

The programme-level discipline (programme objectives, resources, competence, risks, monitoring, review) is distinct from the engagement-level discipline (plan, perform, report, follow up).

### The engagement cycle (clause 6)

ISO 19011 clause 6 walks through the audit engagement cycle. Each step maps to specific workpaper deliverables:

- **Initiating the audit** — contact with the auditee, feasibility, agreement on objectives, scope, criteria.
- **Preparing audit activities** — document review, audit plan, work assignment to audit team, preparation of work documents.
- **Conducting the audit activities** — opening meeting, review during the audit, auditor communication, roles of guides/observers, collection and verification of information, formulation of findings, preparation of audit conclusions, closing meeting.
- **Preparing and distributing the audit report** — report content per clause 6.6.
- **Completion of the audit** — distribution and retention.
- **Audit follow-up** — if included in the engagement, follow-up on actions.

### Auditor competence (clause 7)

ISO 19011 clause 7 sets the competence framework for auditors — education, work experience, auditor training, audit experience, personal behaviour, knowledge and skills (both generic and management-system-specific), continual professional development. For AI management-system audits this is where the auditor's AI-specific competence is pinned — the certification body is expected to maintain evidence that lead auditors on AIMS audits have AI subject-matter competence (see ISO/IEC 42006 below).

## ISO/IEC 17021-1:2015 — requirements for management-system certification bodies

Where ISO 19011 is guidance, **ISO/IEC 17021-1:2015** is the **requirement** standard for any body issuing certifications of management systems — ISO 9001, 14001, 27001, 42001. An accreditation body (UKAS, ANAB, DAkkS, JAS-ANZ etc., operating under IAF MLA) accredits certification bodies against 17021-1; auditors working for those CBs execute against 17021-1 directly.

### Structure you need to be able to recite

- **Clause 4 — Principles.** Impartiality, competence, responsibility, openness, confidentiality, responsiveness to complaints, risk-based approach.
- **Clause 5 — General requirements.** Legal and contractual matters, management of impartiality, liability and financing.
- **Clause 6 — Structural requirements.** Organisational structure and top management, mechanism for safeguarding impartiality.
- **Clause 7 — Resource requirements.** Competence of personnel; personnel involved in the certification activities; use of individual external auditors and external technical experts; personnel records; outsourcing.
- **Clause 8 — Information requirements.** Public information; certification documents; reference to certification and use of marks; directory of certified clients; confidentiality; information exchange between the CB and its clients.
- **Clause 9 — Process requirements.** Pre-certification activities; planning audits (including determination of audit time); initial certification; surveillance activities; recertification; special audits; suspending, withdrawing or reducing scope; appeals; complaints; client records.
- **Clause 10 — Management-system requirements for certification bodies.**

### Impartiality — the 17021-1 flagship

Clause 5.2 is the impartiality discipline. The CB must have **top-management commitment to impartiality**, must identify, analyse, and document impartiality risks on an ongoing basis (an **impartiality committee** is one way, named explicitly in clause 5.2), and must act to eliminate or minimise identified threats. Threats explicitly named include self-interest, self-review, familiarity (trust), and intimidation — which matches the IFAC Code of Ethics and the IESBA framework the IAASB/AICPA stack uses, so the same threat vocabulary works across all your engagements.

For an AIMS auditor this has two bite points:

- The CB cannot have provided management-system consultancy to the client within 2 years prior to the certification audit (clause 5.2.5 and related guidance in ISO/IEC 17021-1 Annex H on time-limited impartiality constraints).
- The individual auditor (not just the CB) must have personal impartiality — the auditor cannot have been employed by or contracted into the auditee's first-line AI engineering work within the equivalent cooling-off window.

### Stage-1 / Stage-2 / surveillance / recertification (clause 9)

ISO/IEC 17021-1 clause 9 defines the certification cycle:

- **Stage 1** — initial audit, performed to understand the management system, review documentation, evaluate the location and site-specific conditions, evaluate the readiness of the auditee for stage 2, plan stage 2, confirm the scope, allocate resources.
- **Stage 2** — on-site evaluation of the implementation and effectiveness of the management system. Audit against the full standard.
- **Certification decision** — made by the CB based on the audit report; the lead auditor does not make the certification decision.
- **Surveillance** — at least annually, confirming continued conformance and improvement.
- **Recertification** — a full recertification audit prior to expiry of the 3-year certification cycle.

### Audit time

Annex B provides audit-time determination (duration in days as a function of organisation size and complexity). AIMS-specific audit time is overlaid by ISO/IEC 42006.

## ISO/IEC 42006 — the AIMS-specific CB requirements

**ISO/IEC 42006** supplements ISO/IEC 17021-1 for bodies providing audit and certification of AI management systems against ISO/IEC 42001. The need for an AIMS-specific CB standard parallels the pattern set by ISO/IEC 27006 for ISMS certification against ISO/IEC 27001.

The topics ISO/IEC 42006 is scoped to cover (and will cover once finalised, per the SC 42 working documents):

- **Competence requirements for AIMS auditors and technical experts** — AI-specific knowledge the lead auditor and audit team must hold (AI lifecycle, AI risk vocabulary, AI technology families, AI-specific regulatory landscape), complementing the generic ISO/IEC 17021-1 clause 7 competence.
- **Audit time** — determination of AIMS audit duration as a function of organisation size, complexity, number of AI systems, AI system risk profile (adjusting ISO/IEC 17021-1 Annex B baselines).
- **Audit methodology specific to AIMS** — sampling of AI systems in scope, handling of multiple AI systems across one AIMS, interaction with product-level AI conformity assessments (EU AI Act, sector regulators), interaction with integrated management-system audits (27001, 9001, 14971).
- **Scope-definition requirements** — defining the AIMS scope precisely enough to support a defensible certification statement (including which AI systems are "in" the AIMS).

An AIMS certification audit proceeds under **ISO/IEC 17021-1 + ISO/IEC 42006 + ISO 19011 guidance + the AIMS criteria (ISO/IEC 42001 clauses 4-10 and Annex A)** — four layers, all of which are cited in the audit plan and report.

## Example — scoping an ISO/IEC 42001 Stage-1 audit

Scenario: a mid-size insurer operates an AIMS covering underwriting AI, fraud-detection AI, and a customer-service chatbot. The organisation has engaged an accredited certification body for ISO/IEC 42001 initial certification. The lead auditor is scoping Stage 1.

Working inputs the lead auditor assembles from the three standards:

- **From ISO 19011** — the engagement-cycle structure (initiating → preparing → conducting → reporting → completion → follow-up), the seven principles, and audit-team competence per clause 7.
- **From ISO/IEC 17021-1** — Stage-1 purpose (documentation review, site conditions, auditee readiness for Stage 2, scope confirmation), audit-time baseline from Annex B, impartiality screen for the audit team and for the CB (any prior consulting? any familiarity threats?).
- **From ISO/IEC 42006** — AIMS-specific audit-time adjustment (three AI systems in scope with mixed inherent risk — scope expansion from baseline), AI-specific competence verification for the lead auditor (AI lifecycle, AI risk vocabulary, AI regulatory landscape).
- **From ISO/IEC 42001** — the AIMS criteria the audit will eventually test (clauses 4-10, Annex A), and Annex B guidance on AIMS implementation that informs documentation review in Stage 1.

Stage 1 output: documentation review memo, readiness opinion for Stage 2, audit-plan draft for Stage 2 (team, time, sampling of AI systems), documented impartiality screen on file at the CB.

## Boundary note — internal AIMS audit under clause 9.2 of ISO/IEC 42001

ISO/IEC 42001 clause 9.2 requires the organisation to conduct **internal audits** of its AIMS at planned intervals. An internal AIMS audit:

- Is **not** a certification audit — it does not involve ISO/IEC 17021-1 or ISO/IEC 42006 (those are CB-side).
- **Does** use ISO 19011 as its guidance.
- **Must** be performed by auditors independent of the AIMS area being audited (clause 9.2.2 reads with ISO 19011 clause 5.4.4 independence).

Internal AIMS audit is the primary artifact an AI Auditor executes inside an organisation preparing for certification. It is covered in depth in `mod-105-auditing-iso-42001-aims`. The three-standard stack here is the shape that *certification-body* auditors execute against the same AIMS, which the internal AIMS audit anticipates.

## Summary

- **ISO 19011:2018** is the horizontal guidelines document for auditing management systems — seven principles, audit programme, engagement cycle, auditor competence.
- **ISO/IEC 17021-1:2015** is the requirements standard for management-system certification bodies — impartiality, competence, Stage-1 / Stage-2 / surveillance / recertification cycle.
- **ISO/IEC 42006** is the AIMS-specific CB standard layered on 17021-1 — AI competence, AIMS audit time, AIMS audit methodology, AIMS scope definition.
- AIMS certification audits run under all three (plus ISO/IEC 42001 as the criteria); internal AIMS audits run under ISO 19011 + ISO/IEC 42001 clause 9.2 without the CB standards.
- The three-standard stack is the shape `mod-105-auditing-iso-42001-aims` builds on.
