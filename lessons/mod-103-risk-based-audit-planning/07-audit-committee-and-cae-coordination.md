# Coordinating the Plan with the Chief Audit Executive and the Audit Committee

## Motivation

Chapters 1 through 6 produced an auditable universe, a scored annual plan, materiality workpapers, scoped engagements, and engagement plans. None of that is useful if it is not *approved* and *maintained* through the cycle. **IIA Global Internal Audit Standards 2024** Domain III (Governing the Internal Audit Function) assigns the audit-committee-interface and plan-approval responsibilities to the **Chief Audit Executive (CAE)**; Domain IV (Managing the Internal Audit Function) assigns resource allocation, reporting cadence, and change management to the CAE; Standard 9.4 explicitly requires the internal audit plan to be approved by the board (operating through the audit committee).

The AI Auditor at level 45 is a **senior practitioner** inside the audit function, not the CAE. The coordination patterns in this chapter flow *up* — from the AI-audit workstream to the CAE, and through the CAE to the audit committee and the audit-committee chair. Mis-reading this boundary is a recurring career failure for strong practitioners: an AI Auditor who starts briefing the audit committee directly, out of cycle, or over the CAE's head is undermining the function's governance line and may be seen as insubordinate even where the content is correct.

> **Role boundary, loud and in writing.** Enterprise audit-function leadership is the CAE's job. Audit-committee chair direct-line is the CAE's and the committee chair's relationship. The AI Auditor supports, briefs, drafts, and sits in meetings as a subject expert — but does not own the relationship. This chapter codifies *what you give the CAE*, not *what you give the audit committee directly*.

## The CAE's enterprise-level accountabilities (so you know what you are feeding)

Under IIA Standards 2024 Domain III the CAE:

- Reports administratively to a senior executive (often the CFO or CEO) and functionally to the board / audit committee.
- Owns an **internal audit charter** approved by the board.
- Owns the **internal audit strategy** and is accountable for its alignment to organisational strategy.
- Owns the **internal audit plan** and submits it for board approval.
- Communicates the audit function's performance to the board (quality assurance, resource adequacy, significant risks, significant findings).
- Interfaces with external auditors and regulators.
- Leads the function's quality assurance and improvement programme under Domain II.

The audit committee under NYSE / Nasdaq / SOX 404 and equivalent listings in other jurisdictions (UK Code, ASX, SGX, HK) is accountable for:

- Oversight of internal audit and external audit.
- Oversight of financial-reporting controls and risk management.
- Approval of the internal audit charter, plan, and significant plan changes.
- Oversight of the CAE's performance and the function's independence.
- Receiving reports from the CAE on significant findings, resource constraints, and scope impairments.

For AI specifically, many audit committees now ask for:

- **AI inventory coverage reporting** — does the organisation have a defensible AI inventory, and does audit have confidence in it?
- **AI risk posture reporting** — residual-risk by AI-system category, trend over time, known exposures to EU AI Act / Colorado / sector regulators.
- **AI plan adequacy** — does audit's annual plan cover the top AI risks with appropriate frequency?
- **AI incident reporting** — have AI-related incidents been handled within the organisation's process; are there systemic issues?
- **External AI assurance coordination** — status of the ISO/IEC 42001 certification path, EU AI Act conformity assessments, SOC-for-AI attestations, regulator inquiries.

The AI Auditor's job is to produce the inputs the CAE needs to address these items. The CAE decides what reaches the committee and in what form.

## Communication protocol — what goes up, when, in what shape

The protocol operates on three cadences: planning cadence (annual), reporting cadence (quarterly), and event-driven escalation.

### Annual planning cadence

Four touchpoints per cycle:

1. **Draft universe + scoring memo** — supplied to the CAE 10–12 weeks before audit-committee approval. Contents: updated universe, scoring methodology (chapter 2), weighting rationale, declined-row list, capacity analysis, resource-gap statement if applicable.
2. **Draft plan review** — CAE and AI Auditor walk the plan; CAE edits for enterprise-plan consistency; AI-audit-specific reshuffles negotiated.
3. **Pre-committee briefing** — CAE presents the plan to the audit-committee chair; AI Auditor may attend as subject expert but does not present.
4. **Audit-committee approval** — CAE presents; AI Auditor available to answer AI-specific questions if invited; plan approved and documented in committee minutes.

Documentation after approval:

- Approved plan — version-locked and published to the function and to senior management.
- Approval date and minuted resolution reference.
- Universe-change log for the prior cycle.
- The weighting document, versioned.

### Quarterly reporting cadence

The CAE reports to the audit committee quarterly (minimum, under common committee charters; monthly or ad-hoc for high-risk functions). The AI-audit input to each report:

- **Plan status** — engagements completed, in-flight, upcoming, deferred; coverage against plan.
- **Significant findings** — AI-specific findings rising to committee-report threshold under the audit function's severity definitions.
- **Management-response status** — AI findings' remediation progress; overdue items.
- **Universe drift** — additions since approval (new AI systems, acquisitions, regulatory-classification changes); any need to re-prioritise.
- **External assurance status** — ISO/IEC 42001 certification path progress, EU AI Act conformity-assessment milestones, SOC-for-AI attestation updates, regulator contacts.
- **Resource flags** — specialist capacity, independence constraints, tooling gaps.
- **Emerging-risk commentary** — new regulatory developments (EU AI Office acts, NIST publications, state AI statutes), new incident types, new third-party exposures.

Shape: a one-to-two-page AI-audit section in the CAE's quarterly committee report. The AI Auditor drafts; the CAE edits and signs.

### Event-driven escalation

Trigger a between-cycle escalation to the CAE when:

- A regulator opens an AI-related inquiry affecting a planned or in-flight engagement.
- An AI incident at the organisation rises to the severity-reporting threshold.
- A planned engagement cannot proceed (auditability has collapsed, independence impaired, specialist lost).
- The responsible party refuses evidence or disputes scope at a level the lead auditor cannot resolve.
- A finding emerges whose implications exceed the engagement — enterprise-wide pattern, systemic AIMS failure, significant management-response failure.
- A whistleblower or tipline item reaches the audit function about AI subject matter.

Each trigger has a documented path (chapter 6 §8). The CAE decides whether and when the audit committee or committee chair is informed.

## Change management on the approved plan

Plans change. ISA 320 paragraph 12 requires the auditor to revise materiality if information suggests it was set wrongly; the plan analogue is similar. Trigger types:

- **Risk-driven** — universe scoring suggests a new engagement is now critical mid-cycle.
- **Capacity-driven** — the function lost a specialist and cannot deliver an engagement on time.
- **External-driven** — the CB scheduled Stage-2 on a different date; the external financial-statement audit shifted; a regulator imposed a deadline.
- **Finding-driven** — an in-flight engagement discovered an issue that points to an adjacent engagement needing to start early.

Change-management process:

1. AI Auditor documents the proposed change, impact, and rationale.
2. CAE reviews; decides whether the change is within the function's delegated authority or requires audit-committee re-approval. Typical delegation lines:
    - Reshuffles within already-approved plan — CAE authority.
    - Adding a new engagement requiring re-prioritisation of a declined row — CAE authority with notification to committee chair.
    - Dropping an approved engagement — committee notification; chair concurrence; committee re-approval at next cycle.
    - Material scope change on an approved engagement — committee notification.
3. CAE updates the plan document and the universe-change log.
4. CAE reports the change at the next quarterly report; material changes are reported immediately.

Documentation: every plan change is logged with trigger, decision owner, decision date, and impact. The audit committee reviews the log at the next cycle.

## External-auditor coordination

The audit committee oversees both internal and external auditors; the CAE coordinates the interface, and the AI Auditor supports. External parties to coordinate with for AI work:

- **Financial-statement external auditor** (Big 4 or mid-tier) — ISA 600 / AU-C 600 group-audit coordination; AT-C 105 attestation coordination for SOC reports. AI subject matter may be *relevant* to the financial-statement audit (impairment of AI assets, regulatory liability accruals, going-concern considerations where regulator action is likely) and the FS auditor has rights to internal audit's work under ISA 610.
- **ISO/IEC 42001 certification body** — Stage-1 and Stage-2 scheduling, coordinated information requests, non-overlap of certification-audit and internal-audit clause coverage, leverage of internal audit evidence under ISO/IEC 42001 clause 9.2.
- **EU AI Act notified body** — for Annex III high-risk systems requiring third-party conformity assessment; coordination of evidence, timing, and disclosure obligations.
- **ForHumanity / BABL AI / SR 11-7 external validator** — specialised AI attestations the organisation holds; the internal audit function may rely on or re-test their work.
- **Regulators** — supervisory interactions (US Fed, OCC, NAIC, NYDFS; FDA for SaMD; FTC, CFPB, EEOC, DOJ, HHS OCR; EU AI Office; EU DPAs; national AI supervisory authorities). The CAE owns the interface; AI Auditor attends as subject expert when requested.

The CAE maintains an **external-assurance map** for the audit committee: which external party covers which subject matter, when, with what scope, and how internal audit uses the coverage. The AI Auditor keeps the AI section of this map current. The map is a quarterly-report attachment.

## Reporting cadence — rhythm, not just content

Beyond the quarterly cadence, high-visibility AI programmes often attract additional rhythms:

- **Monthly CAE check-in** on AI-audit workstream — plan status, universe drift, early-warning signals.
- **Weekly fieldwork stand-up** during in-flight engagements — the AI Auditor runs this; CAE joins by exception.
- **Annual deep-dive to the audit committee** on AI risk posture — once per cycle the committee receives a longer AI-focused briefing from the CAE with the AI Auditor in support.
- **Ad-hoc regulator-response briefings** — when a regulator contacts the organisation on AI subject matter.

The cadence is proposed by the CAE and endorsed by the audit-committee chair; the AI Auditor's job is to supply inputs reliably within the cadence.

## Deferring up — the non-negotiable list

There is a set of decisions the AI Auditor never makes unilaterally; they go to the CAE or the audit-committee chair:

- Final approval of the annual plan.
- Approval of material scope changes mid-cycle.
- Resource allocation across the enterprise audit function (AI vs. other workstreams).
- External-auditor interface decisions.
- Regulator-communication decisions.
- Public disclosure of findings or audit outputs.
- Audit-function strategy and charter revisions.
- Audit-function quality-assurance and improvement programme decisions.
- Hiring of audit staff and specialists.
- The AI-audit workstream's position within the function (reporting, structure, budget).

Each is a CAE or committee-chair call. The AI Auditor's role is to brief clearly, recommend, and execute the decision — not to make or pre-empt it.

## What the CAE expects the AI Auditor to own

In return the AI Auditor owns, without referring up:

- The content and defensibility of the AI audit universe, scoring methodology, and engagement scope memos.
- The quality of fieldwork, workpaper discipline, and evidence standards within engagements.
- The relationship with first- and second-line AI stakeholders at the practitioner level (ML engineers, risk engineers, evaluation engineers, governance analysts, architects).
- The timely surfacing of issues to the CAE through the documented escalation paths.
- The currency of the audit function's AI-specific competence (reading new standards, tracking the regulatory landscape, maintaining specialist networks).
- Documentation of all of the above so the CAE and the audit committee can see the work.

## Common failure modes

- **Going around the CAE.** The AI Auditor briefs the audit-committee chair directly (or worse, a non-audit-committee board member). The CAE loses control of the function's narrative; relationships rupture.
- **Under-informing the CAE.** The AI Auditor runs engagements without routine upward reporting; the CAE is surprised by findings at committee time.
- **Over-reaching into enterprise decisions.** The AI Auditor proposes external-auditor coordination changes or regulator-interface decisions unilaterally; the CAE and committee are blindsided.
- **Informal plan changes.** Mid-cycle reshuffles are made without documentation; the audit committee's approved plan is quietly diverged from.
- **External-assurance map goes stale.** Nobody keeps the map current; the audit committee cannot see where AI assurance comes from and gaps are not visible.
- **Quarterly inputs delivered late.** The CAE has to fill the AI section from memory; the committee report loses specificity.
- **Specialist capacity gap goes unreported.** The function cannot deliver a planned engagement; the audit committee learns from the quarterly report that the plan is slipping instead of from a resource ask three months earlier.

## Summary

- Audit-committee and CAE coordination is a **flow-up** discipline — the AI Auditor supplies inputs; the CAE owns the committee interface. Crossing this boundary is a career-level failure even when the content is right.
- The protocol runs on three cadences: annual planning (universe → scoring → plan → committee approval), quarterly reporting (plan status, findings, universe drift, external assurance, resource flags), and event-driven escalation (regulator contact, incident, auditability loss, suspected fraud, enterprise-wide finding).
- Change management on the approved plan has explicit delegation lines — within-plan reshuffles vs. committee-notification-required vs. committee-re-approval.
- External-auditor coordination (FS auditor, certification body, notified body, regulators, specialised validators) is the CAE's interface; the AI Auditor maintains the AI section of the external-assurance map.
- The AI Auditor's own authority runs to engagement-level content and quality; enterprise-level decisions defer up to the CAE and the audit-committee chair.
- Documentation — approved-plan versions, universe-change logs, external-assurance maps, quarterly reports — is the audit-committee-facing evidence that the function is governed, not just operating.
