# exercise-01: ISA 500 Evidence Sufficiency and Appropriateness Drill

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Given a short engagement scenario and a list of candidate evidence items management has handed over, grade each item under the ISA 500 / AU-C 500 reliability generalisations, decide whether the evidence stack is *sufficient* and *appropriate* for the key assertion, and specify the additional procedures required to close the gaps. The deliverable is an evidence-evaluation memo the engagement partner would read on a Monday morning.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [`01-isa-500-evidence-applied-to-ai-artifacts.md`](../01-isa-500-evidence-applied-to-ai-artifacts.md)
- `mod-101` chapter 5 (IAASB + AICPA attestation stack)
- `mod-102` chapter 1 (what makes a criterion auditable) — needed to map evidence to the criterion

## Scenario

**Meridian Health Systems** operates an LLM-based clinical-note summarisation assistant integrated into its electronic health record (EHR) system. The engagement is a limited-assurance ISAE 3000 / AT-C 210 examination for the organisation's regulatory affairs function, covering the operating effectiveness of the following control:

> "Production outputs of the clinical-note summarisation assistant are generated by the model version, prompt template, and guardrail configuration formally approved by the AI Governance Committee for clinical use during the audit period 2026-01-01 through 2026-09-30."

This is the key assertion for this workpaper.

Management has handed over the following evidence items:

1. **A model card** on the model owner's wiki page, last edited 2026-10-02, listing the model name "clin-sum-llm v4.2" and a bullet-list summary of training data.
2. **A screenshot of the Grafana "AI Health" dashboard** showing a chart labelled "approved model version" at 100% for every day in the period.
3. **A Google Doc meeting-minutes export** from the AI Governance Committee's 2026-01-14 approval of v4.2 for clinical use, with no attached signatures.
4. **A verbal confirmation during an interview** with the AI lead that no guardrail-config change occurred during the period.
5. **A CSV export** from the model registry showing one row per deployment event, with columns `timestamp, model_name, model_version, deployed_by`. The registry is MLflow instance hosted on an internal VM; the export was produced by the AI lead on 2026-10-15.
6. **A SOC 2 Type II report** from the organisation's cloud infrastructure provider covering 2025-06-01 to 2026-05-31, with the "Change Management" trust-services criteria in scope.
7. **An email thread** between the AI lead and the AI Governance Committee chair confirming the committee reviewed and approved v4.2.
8. **The organisation's management representation letter**, signed on the final day of fieldwork, stating that all approved model versions were deployed and that no unapproved deployments occurred during the period.

Additionally, the engagement team has not yet reached:

- The underlying MLflow registry database itself (only the CSV export is held).
- The IaC repository that manages the production deployment manifests.
- The guardrail-config file history (the engagement team has not asked yet).
- The inference-trace store holding per-request model-version tags.

## Requirements

1. **Grade each of the eight evidence items** against the ISA 500 reliability generalisations. For each: state the tier (original / derivative / corroborative, from chapter 4), state the reliability grade A / B / C / D, and name the generalisation(s) that pin the grade. One paragraph per item.
2. **Map each item to the key assertion.** For each evidence item, state whether it is *relevant to the assertion*, *partially relevant*, or *not relevant*. Explain. (Several items are not relevant to this assertion; that is a planning finding against the hand-over, not a failing of the item.)
3. **Diagnose the sufficiency gap.** With only the eight items, is the evidence stack sufficient and appropriate for a limited-assurance conclusion on the key assertion? Explain explicitly, naming which parts of the assertion are and are not supported.
4. **Specify additional procedures.** Write a numbered list of the additional procedures required to close the gap (chapter 1 §Common failure modes should be prompting your thinking). For each procedure, identify: the target artifact, the control the procedure tests, the ISA 500 reliability generalisation that upgrades the evidence, and the expected deliverable in the workpaper.
5. **Draft the one-page evidence-evaluation memo** addressed to the engagement partner. The memo should state: the key assertion, the current evidence stack with grades, the sufficiency conclusion, the additional procedures required, and the risk to the engagement timeline if the model owner does not cooperate.

## Starter guidance

- The model card (item 1) is a derivative artifact in nearly every engagement; treat it as a map to the originals, not as evidence of the claim.
- The dashboard screenshot (item 2) and the CSV export (item 5) are both *representations* of the same underlying data (the model registry); they should be reconciled to the registry itself.
- The SOC 2 Type II (item 6) is corroborative *for the scope it covers*; carefully read its period and subject-matter scope before relying on it for the clinical AI change-management control.
- The management representation letter (item 8) is required under ISA 580 / AU-C 580 but is *not substantive evidence* of the represented control; it memorialises management's assertion.
- The verbal confirmation (item 4) is weaker than any documentary alternative; the discipline is to reduce it to a documentary artifact.
- Several of the gaps in §Scope (MLflow DB, IaC repo, guardrail history, trace-store) are the obvious next procedures.
- Where the scenario does not specify a detail you would want (e.g., whether MLflow runs on a WORM-backed store, whether the trace-store retains model-version tags for the full period), use `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` placeholders rather than invent.

## Acceptance criteria

The deliverable is accepted when:

- Every one of the eight evidence items has a tier, a grade, a relevance verdict, and a generalisation citation.
- The sufficiency diagnosis names *specifically* which parts of the assertion are unsupported (not a vague "insufficient evidence").
- The additional procedures include at least one auditor-direct procedure (independent re-performance, direct query, observation) and at least one procedure that reaches the Git-tracked original (IaC repo, guardrail-config history).
- The additional procedures include a reconciliation between the CSV export (item 5) and the MLflow registry itself.
- The SOC 2 Type II reliance is evaluated against period and subject-matter scope, not accepted uncritically.
- The memo distinguishes between the ISA 580 representation (item 8) and substantive evidence.
- The memo names the ISA 500 generalisation(s) driving each upgrade recommendation.
- `<!-- needs-research -->` placeholders appear where the scenario legitimately underspecifies a detail.
- No fabricated vendor names, regulators, or statutes beyond what the scenario provides.

## Stretch goals

- Draft the **evidence-request list** that the engagement would send the model owner to close the gaps, in the shape of a professional information-request letter (date, requestor, items, format required, response deadline, confidentiality reminder).
- Write a short **professional-skepticism note** reflecting on item 1 (the model card, last edited 2026-10-02) — the edit date is after the audit period ends; what questions does that raise, and what procedure would you run to address them?
- Propose the **sampling frame** you would use if the engagement needed to test a sample of deployment events; refer forward to chapter 2 / exercise-02.
